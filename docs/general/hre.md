# The HRE

Rulebook references are to §18 (pp.44 to 45), third printing (2026).

## Defending the HRE (§18.4, p.44) 🔵
While the Emperor has the **Defending the HRE** slot tagged, an external Aggressor may never resolve peace separately with an NPR Imperial Subject unless they have a Total or Partial Victory over the Emperor. Otherwise they resolve peace with the Emperor, treating NPR Imperial Subjects at war with the Emperor's enemies as his Active Allies. The Emperor's response covers **external Aggressors**, meaning players with a War token on his Capital, not Realms the Emperor himself declared war on (🟢 Mikhs, Mar 2026). See [Wars and Truces](wars-and-truces.md).

## Units defending an HRE Area (table, p.45)
Calculate **two things separately** (🟢 Mikhs, 5 Oct 2026):
1. The **MC the NPR has in the Area**. This is the floor and is never reduced.
2. The Emperor's bonus, **3 × Imperial Authority − 2 × the number of HRE Areas containing non-HRE Units prior to this Turn**. If it is **positive**, add it. If it is negative, nothing happens.

Examples: MC 3 with a bonus of −3 gives **3** Units. MC 3 with a bonus of +3 gives **6**. The figure is worked out **once per Area**, however many NPRs or Provinces are attacked there (🟢 Mikhs, 11 Oct 2026). A common misreading is to subtract the bonus rows from the MC.

## Elector Areas and Vassals (p.44 to 45)
- An Elector Area must contain the Capital of at least 1 HRE Member.
- A **Vassalized Elector Area still counts** and votes for its **Overlord** if eligible (🟢 Mikhs, 13 Oct 2026; 🔵 p.45: an Elector Area with no NPR Provinces where all Vassal tokens belong to a single PR votes for that PR).
- Eligibility for election: follow the HRE's official Religion (if any), and the PR isn't in an Interregnum. Imperial Elections are labelled Advanced Rules.

## An Emperor who changes religion
A thread on Marriages and religion (Oct 2026) used the Emperor as an analogy: by game mechanics a converted Emperor stays Emperor but isn't eligible at the next election (🟡 Miroku20X6, consistent with p.45). Ideas such as triggering a new election are 🟣 houserules.
