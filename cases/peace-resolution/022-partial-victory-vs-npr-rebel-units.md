# Partial Victory against an NPR: what is counted

## The question
Against an NPR or Bot, Partial Victory needs your Deployed Land Units to outnumber **the total number of [Rebel Unit icon] in their Areas** plus any Deployed Enemy Land Units. NPRs never have Deployed Land Units, so what is counted?

## Answer (🟡 damifoe, supported by the text)
1. NPRs have **no Deployed Units**. The only NPR units on the map are the **NPR Invasion** Units, and after the Invasion they behave as **Rebel Units**.
2. So the Victor needs more Deployed Land Units than there are **Rebel Units in the Victor's own Areas** (damifoe reads "their Areas" that way).
3. The icon in the table looks identical to the Rebel Unit icon in the Invasion text ("From this point onwards, these NPR Units behave as [Rebel Unit]"). That visual match is my reading and should be checked against the printed page.

## Invasions
- Place NPR Units equal to **½ of the current NPR Strength**, ignoring Provinces in Areas with Units Hostile to the NPR.
- 🟡 Miroku20X6: **NPR Strength isn't bound by adjacency** like Military Capacity. It is the unoccupied Realm's Tax Value plus its Vassals. ryssen2's group had played Invasions wrongly because of this. The DNPR token can hide that a Province is Large (Halab counts as 7, not 6).

## Balance discussion, in brief
Phil: NPRs exist as fodder for PRs, not as a challenge. Miroku showed the costs: Royal Hungary means fighting 8 Units, then an Invasion of 4, for 3 Towns. Bogdan: NPRs retreat outside their Capital if outnumbered after a Round, so large NPRs are easier than they look.

## Table houserule (🟣 not official)
Bogdan suggested only retreating if you double the NPR's remaining army, or only if you outnumber it after 2 Rounds.
