# Partial Victory first, Total Victory second

## Setup (as posted)
France and England were both at war with **Burgundy**. France occupied the capitals plus Artois and Luxembourg and enforced a Partial Victory with **Vassalization** on its turn. England occupied **Amsterdam, Zeeland and Breda** in the Netherlands, which left it occupying every Province of **Holland** and **Brabant**. *(The thread doesn't spell out how Holland and Brabant relate to Burgundy; check the board before using this as a template.)*

## The question
England was "already in the Partial Victory step". Does it still have a Total Victory over Holland and Brabant?

## Answer
**Yes.**
- 🟢 **Mikhs:** you can. If a remaining war turns out to be a Total Victory, it is resolved then, it doesn't wait for the next Round, and Total-Victory-only terms such as Full Annexation are usable.
- 🟡 **Atlas:** being in step 3 doesn't change whether you have Total Victory. England simply couldn't resolve it in step 2, so it resolves it now, because step 3 covers "all other peace deals". England makes peace with Holland and Brabant **separately**.
- Turn Order matters: if England had come **before** France, it would not yet have had the Total Victory (Atlas).

## The disagreement, in brief
Čačka argued that Total Victory is determined **only** in step 2 and that the flow chart is misleading. Mikhs and Atlas answered that step 3 is "all remaining wars", not "all remaining Partial Victories". On 4 Jun 2026 Čačka withdrew the objection. Čačka suggested a sentence for Step 3 ("a Total Victory may occur again; resolve it as described above"), which is **their proposal, not official**. Atlas noted it would change the rule by making such Total Victories mandatory in step 3, whereas step 3 is optional.

## Status notes
Principle 🟢 (Mikhs, who says the designer explained it the same way). Details of this board 🟡.
