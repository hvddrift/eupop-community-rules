# Two PRs at war with one NPR, one Province each

## Setup
| | |
|---|---|
| Oceres | PR, at war with the NPR **Papacy**. Occupies **Rome and Ancona**. |
| Oceres's PR ally | Occupies **Avignon** |

## The question
How does peace go, and can Oceres Full Annex?

## Resolution
1. 🟢 **Eivind (designer):** "Two separate Peace Resolutions. In Turn order."
2. 🟢 **Mikhs:** Oceres **cannot** enforce Full Annexation, because Total Victory needs **one PR** to occupy **all** of the NPR's de jure Provinces. The ally's Province doesn't count towards Oceres.
3. 🟡 **sim-h:** the order matters. If the ally resolves first and Avignon stops being a Papal Province in that peace, Oceres can then annex what is left, **if** the ally's term removes Avignon from the Papacy's de jure set. Work out what the ally's term does to Avignon before assuming this.

*Allies only resolve jointly when the ally is an **NPR Active Ally** (see [PR-016](016-two-allied-prs-one-human-enemy.md)).*

## Table houserule (🟣 not official)
Chant asked why turn order should matter when **all** Provinces are occupied. The designer's answer stands: the Provinces are resolved separately. Several experienced players would **allow a joint annexation as a houserule**, mainly between allies or in team play (Miroku20X6, insearchofescapism, damifoe). Atlas would allow it only if the table agrees. Ames suggested "if both players agree". Shadoune's view of the design reason: turn order creates player-to-player diplomacy, and ways to hurt other players in a war.

## Status notes
🟢 for the two rulings. The houserule is explicitly **not** the rules.
